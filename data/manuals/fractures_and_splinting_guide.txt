FRACTURES & SUSPECTED BROKEN BONES — QUICK FIRST AID GUIDE (OFFLINE)

DISCLAIMER
- This guide is educational. It is NOT a substitute for professional medical care.
- If the person is in immediate danger, has major bleeding, is unconscious, or you suspect a head/neck/spine injury, call emergency services immediately.

GOALS (WHAT YOU’RE TRYING TO DO)
1) Prevent further injury by immobilizing the area.
2) Protect circulation and nerves (blood flow + sensation).
3) Control pain and swelling safely.
4) Identify emergencies (open fracture, poor circulation, spinal injury, shock).


1) HOW TO RECOGNIZE A FRACTURE
A fracture can be present even without obvious deformity.
Common signs:
- Pain at a specific spot, worse with movement or weight bearing
- Swelling and tenderness, bruising
- Deformity (bone looks bent/angled), limb looks shorter/rotated
- Inability to use the limb normally (can’t bear weight, can’t move without severe pain)
- Clicking/grating sensation (do NOT test for this)
- Numbness/tingling, coldness, pale/blue skin beyond the injury

SPECIAL EMERGENCY SIGNS
- Open fracture: bone ends visible or a deep wound over the fracture site
- Severe bleeding that won’t stop with pressure
- No pulse, very cold/blue hand or foot below injury, or worsening numbness
- Suspected spine injury: neck/back pain after trauma, weakness, numbness, confusion
- Severe pain with tight swelling after injury (possible compartment syndrome)


2) FIRST THINGS FIRST (SAFETY + QUICK CHECKS)
A) Scene safety
- Make sure you and the patient are safe (traffic, glass, unstable debris, etc.).

B) Quick “ABCs”
- Airway: are they able to speak?
- Breathing: normal breathing?
- Circulation: major bleeding? pale/clammy? dizzy?

If major bleeding is present, control it FIRST (direct pressure, wound packing, tourniquet if trained and needed). A fracture can wait; bleeding can’t.

C) Check circulation and nerves BELOW the injury (do this BEFORE and AFTER splinting)
- Color/warmth: compare to the other side
- Capillary refill: press nail bed until white, release (should return pink within ~2 seconds)
- Pulse: if you know how, feel distal pulse (wrist for arm injuries, top of foot for leg injuries)
- Sensation: can they feel light touch?
- Movement: can they wiggle fingers/toes?

If circulation is compromised (cold/blue/pale, no pulse, worsening numbness), call emergency services now.


3) WHAT NOT TO DO
- Do NOT try to “push the bone back in” or straighten a limb if it causes severe pain or resistance.
- Do NOT force a joint to move.
- Do NOT allow the person to eat/drink if surgery might be needed.
- Do NOT remove an embedded object (stabilize it in place).


4) IMMOBILIZATION (SPLINTING) — THE CORE SKILL
Aim: prevent movement at the fracture site.
General rules:
- Splint in the position found unless circulation is compromised.
- Immobilize the bone and the joints above and below the suspected fracture if possible.
- Pad bony areas to prevent pressure sores.
- Secure the splint firmly but not so tight it cuts off circulation.

STEP-BY-STEP SPLINTING (ANY LIMB)
1) Ask them to stay still. Support the injured limb with your hands.
2) Remove rings/watches/bracelets on the injured side (swelling can trap them).
3) Pad: use clothing, towels, blankets around the injury.
4) Apply a rigid support:
   - Commercial splint, cardboard, rolled magazine, straight stick, folded blanket, etc.
5) Secure with ties:
   - Bandages, cloth strips, belts, scarves. Tie above and below the injury.
   - Avoid tying directly over the painful spot if possible.
6) Re-check distal circulation and sensation.
   - If worse: loosen the ties slightly and re-check.

IMPROVISED SLINGS
- Arm/forearm/wrist: use a triangular bandage or a shirt as a sling.
- Secure arm against the chest with a swathe (wrap) for extra stability.


5) OPEN FRACTURES (BONE THROUGH SKIN)
This is an emergency. Call emergency services.
- Control bleeding with gentle pressure around the wound.
- Do NOT push bone back under the skin.
- Cover with a clean dressing. If available, use a sterile dressing.
- Splint the limb to prevent movement.
- Monitor for shock (pale, cold, fast pulse, confusion).


6) COMMON FRACTURE SCENARIOS
A) Suspected wrist/forearm fracture (fall on outstretched hand)
- Use a rigid splint from hand to below the elbow.
- Keep wrist neutral if possible.
- Sling + swathe to immobilize.

B) Suspected ankle fracture
- Remove shoe only if it comes off easily and you can do it without pain/worsening deformity.
- Pad well, splint the ankle/foot and lower leg.
- Do not let them walk.

C) Suspected femur (thigh) fracture
- High risk of internal bleeding and shock.
- Call emergency services.
- Keep them still; do not attempt to straighten.
- Stabilize leg with padding and secure to the uninjured leg if needed.

D) Suspected rib fracture
- Encourage slow breathing; pain can make breathing shallow.
- Seek urgent care if: shortness of breath, severe chest pain, coughing blood, or worsening breathing.

E) Suspected hip fracture (older adult fall)
- Call emergency services.
- Keep still, keep warm.
- Do not try to stand or walk.


7) PAIN + SWELLING CONTROL
- Ice pack wrapped in cloth (15–20 min on, repeat). Do not place ice directly on skin.
- Elevate the injured limb if it doesn’t increase pain.
- Pain meds (only if safe for the person):
  - Acetaminophen/paracetamol is often safest.
  - Avoid ibuprofen/NSAIDs if there is major bleeding, suspected kidney issues, or if a clinician advised against them.


8) WHEN TO CALL EMERGENCY SERVICES (CALL NOW)
- Open fracture
- Deformity with severe pain, or bone ends visible
- Poor circulation or numbness/weakness beyond the injury
- Suspected head/neck/spine injury
- Suspected femur/hip fracture
- Severe bleeding, or signs of shock
- The person cannot safely move or you cannot splint effectively


9) MONITORING UNTIL HELP ARRIVES
- Check breathing and responsiveness.
- Keep them warm (blanket/jacket).
- Re-check circulation/sensation every 10–15 minutes.
- If symptoms worsen, treat as emergency and escalate care.


QUICK SUMMARY (60 SECONDS)
- Don’t move it.
- Control bleeding.
- Check circulation and sensation below injury.
- Splint it (pad + rigid support + ties).
- Re-check circulation.
- Call for help if open fracture, numb/cold limb, severe deformity, hip/femur, or shock.
