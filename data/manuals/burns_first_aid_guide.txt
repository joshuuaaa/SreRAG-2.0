BURNS — QUICK FIRST AID GUIDE (THERMAL / CHEMICAL / ELECTRICAL)

DISCLAIMER
- This guide is educational and does not replace professional medical care.
- If the person is in immediate danger, call your local emergency number.

GOALS
1) Stop the burning process.
2) Cool the burn safely (thermal burns) or flush chemicals (chemical burns).
3) Protect the burn and prevent infection.
4) Recognize emergencies: airway burns, large/deep burns, electrical burns, chemical burns, shock.


1) QUICKLY ASSESS: IS THIS AN EMERGENCY?
CALL EMERGENCY SERVICES NOW if any of these are true:
- Trouble breathing, hoarse voice, coughing, soot around mouth/nose (possible airway burn)
- Burns to face, airway, hands, feet, genitals, major joints
- Large burns: bigger than the person’s palm (rough guide) OR multiple areas
- Deep burns: white/charred/leathery skin, numbness, severe blistering over a large area
- Electrical burn (even if skin looks okay) or lightning strike
- Chemical burn, especially with eye exposure
- Signs of shock: pale/clammy skin, confusion, fainting, fast weak pulse
- Child, older adult, or person with major medical issues AND burn seems significant


2) THERMAL BURNS (HEAT / FLAME / HOT LIQUID / STEAM)
A) Stop the burn source
- Move away from heat.
- Put out flames (STOP, DROP, ROLL).
- Remove hot/wet clothing if it isn’t stuck to the skin.

B) Cool the burn
- Cool with cool running water for 10–20 minutes (best within first 3 hours).
- If running water isn’t available: cool compresses.
- Do NOT use ice directly (can worsen tissue injury).

C) Remove constricting items
- Remove rings, watches, tight clothing near the burn before swelling increases.

D) Cover and protect
- Cover with a clean, non-stick dressing (sterile if possible).
- Plastic wrap/cling film can be used loosely (do not wrap tightly).

E) Pain control
- Oral pain relief if safe for the person (e.g., acetaminophen/paracetamol).
- Encourage sips of water if fully awake and not nauseated (unless surgery likely).


3) BLISTERS: WHAT TO DO
- Do NOT pop blisters.
- If blister breaks: gently rinse with clean water, cover with non-stick dressing.


4) WHAT NOT TO DO (COMMON MISTAKES)
- No butter/oils/toothpaste/cream “home remedies” on fresh burns.
- No ice directly on burn.
- Don’t peel off clothing stuck to the burn.
- Don’t pop blisters.


5) CHEMICAL BURNS
A) Protect yourself first
- Wear gloves if available; avoid getting chemicals on you.

B) Remove dry chemicals
- Brush off dry powders before adding water.

C) Flush with lots of water
- Rinse skin with running water for at least 20 minutes.
- Remove contaminated clothing/jewelry while flushing.

D) Eyes
- Eye exposure is an emergency. Rinse continuously with clean water/saline and seek urgent care.

Note: For some industrial chemicals, specific instructions exist — if you know the chemical, call poison control.


6) ELECTRICAL BURNS
Electrical burns can cause hidden internal injury and dangerous heart rhythm problems.
- Call emergency services.
- Do NOT touch the person until power is off.
- Once safe: check responsiveness and breathing.
- Start CPR if not breathing normally and call for help.
- Cover visible burns with clean dressing.


7) CLASSIC BURN “DEPTH” QUICK GUIDE (SIMPLIFIED)
- Superficial (1st degree): red, painful, dry, no blisters (like mild sunburn)
- Partial thickness (2nd degree): blisters, wet/shiny, very painful
- Full thickness (3rd degree): white/charred/leathery, may be numb

Depth can be hard to judge — when unsure, treat as more serious and seek care.


8) WHEN TO GET MEDICAL CARE (URGENT)
- Increasing redness, swelling, pus, foul smell, fever
- Worsening pain after initial improvement
- Burn not improving over 2–3 days
- Any burn in babies/young children that is more than very small


QUICK SUMMARY (60 SECONDS)
- Stop the burn.
- Cool with running water 10–20 minutes (no ice).
- Remove rings/tight items.
- Cover with clean non-stick dressing.
- Don’t pop blisters.
- Call emergency services for airway/face/hands/feet/genitals, large/deep burns, chemical/electrical burns, or shock.
